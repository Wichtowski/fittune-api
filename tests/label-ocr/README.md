# Nutrition label OCR validation

Phase 1 uses manual cropping, explicit local/server/AI actions and one authenticated Rust parser.
OCR only proposes values and never saves a product automatically.
Images stay in form/request memory and are neither stored in RustFS nor written to server disk.
The AI request sends the sanitized crop and recognized text, with `store: false` and a strict schema.
OpenAI may retain API data under its provider policy.

## Fixture corpus

`manifest.json` fixes 60 public Open Food Facts photos, 30 Polish and 30 English.
The development split has 20 photos per language; held-out validation has 10 per language.
There are 40 readable and 20 challenging labels across the corpus.
Photos, crop rectangles, printed-value annotations, source URLs, attribution and SHA-256 digests are fixed in the manifest.
Packaging groups stay in one split, including duplicate photos of the same label.
Ground truth was visually reviewed against the printed photos, rather than copied from a product database or generated by OCR.
Null means absent, unreadable, a printed inequality/trace, or an unestablished basis.
US sodium is not silently converted into salt.
The manifest and derived OCR observations are shared under CC-BY-SA-3.0 with attribution to Open Food Facts contributors.

Sources: [dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/openfoodfacts/nutrient-detection-layout), [image licensing](https://openfoodfacts.github.io/openfoodfacts-server/api/tutorials/license-be-on-the-legal-side/).

## Measured baseline

`baseline.json` records the engine settings, resource limits and all four development preprocessing comparisons.
`results/` records every original-crop development and held-out result, including missing and incorrect values.
The archived baseline and results below used the earlier strict tolerance of 1 kcal and the greater of 0.01 g or 2 percent for nutrients, before decimal correction.
They are not directly comparable with future soft-scored runs.
The two normalized public observations in `observations/` are real Polish/English parser regression inputs and inherit the manifest attribution and license.
Held-out labels were evaluated after development tuning; the later kJ/kcal warning does not change extracted values.

| Engine / split | PL correct / printed | EN correct / printed | Incorrect suggestions | PL / EN p95 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RapidOCR development | 57 / 145 | 68 / 129 | 22 | 1.80 s / 1.39 s |
| RapidOCR held-out | 41 / 74 | 17 / 72 | 14 | 1.86 s / 1.45 s |
| Tesseract development | 9 / 145 | 19 / 129 | 30 | 2.53 s / 1.22 s |
| Tesseract held-out | 2 / 74 | 0 / 72 | 7 | 1.86 s / 1.49 s |

Across all 60 photos, RapidOCR populated 183 of 420 printed values correctly and made 36 incorrect suggestions out of 219 populated values.
Tesseract populated 30 of 420 correctly and made 37 incorrect suggestions out of 67 populated values.
These results are insufficient for production reliability, especially for local OCR; they must remain visible review/release blockers.
RapidOCR grayscale added four correct development values but also nine additional incorrect suggestions, while Otsu and Sauvola lost correct values in both languages.
Tesseract preprocessing did not produce a consistent improvement across both languages without additional incorrect suggestions.
Original crops remain the default rather than selecting a transform based only on recall.
The final one-CPU, 512 MiB sidecar completed 180 fixture requests without worker failures or OOMs, with a measured cgroup peak of 370491392 bytes (353.3 MiB).
These timings and memory figures come from a Ryzen 7 3700X desktop with container CPU limits, not the production VPS or a phone.

Full images, crops, observations, reports and logs are kept under `/home/oshki/Projects/fittune/data`, with the new run in `data/label-ocr-rerun`.
The previous observations were also rescored with that rule to separate scoring changes from extraction changes.

| Engine | Correct / printed | Incorrect suggestions | Correct / populated suggestions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RapidOCR | 188 / 420 | 31 | 85.8 percent |
| Tesseract | 35 / 420 | 32 | 52.2 percent |

These scores match the previous output under the same soft rule; the increase over the archived strict baseline comes from scoring tolerance.
The initial macro heuristic introduced one incorrect proposal when `14g` was recognized as `149`; requiring a recognized mass unit prevents that case.
No corrections were applied to this corpus after the guard, so the heuristic has not demonstrated a real-fixture accuracy improvement.
For example, the Tesseract `2,5g` to `25g` error cannot be corrected reliably when protein is missing and kcal are marked uncertain.
Both engines completed all 60 images without service failures; the sidecar's existing cgroup peak remained 353.3 MiB with no OOMs.
The full Rust suite passed 190 tests and the frontend suite passed 198 tests, followed by eight focused OCR unit tests and five OCR API tests after the guard change.
The Python scoring and watchdog checks, Clippy, frontend lint, typecheck and build also passed.
These results continue to block production release on accuracy.

## Macro consistency correction

The shared Rust parser can propose a lost-decimal correction when detected kcal and all three macros are present and the calorie reading has no uncertainty or conflict warning.
It compares 4 kcal per gram of protein/carbohydrate and 9 kcal per gram of fat, using the existing consistency tolerance of 15 percent of detected kcal or 10 kcal, whichever is greater.
The corrected field must also contain an explicitly recognized mass unit, so a lost `g` read as `9` cannot be treated as a missing decimal.
It tries dividing one macro by 10 or 100 and accepts only one unique candidate that also passes supported ranges, total mass and parent-nutrient checks.
The original OCR evidence is preserved, the adjustment is annotated, and the UI warns the user to confirm the printed label.
When detected kcal exceed the original macro estimate by more than that tolerance, correction is skipped and a warning is shown.
This is a proposal for OCR output, not a general way to infer missing nutrients, and AI extraction is not automatically rewritten.
The energy equation is underdetermined and fibre, polyols and other energy contributors can affect the estimate.
The soft-scored rerun above evaluates this heuristic, while the archived strict baseline predates it.

## Release checks and later phases

Run Rust unit/API tests, the Python watchdog check, frontend lint/typecheck/tests/build, and the production Compose configuration check.
Before production rollout, verify p95 server extraction at or below 10 seconds on the actual one-vCPU VPS with its 512 MiB sidecar limit and no worker OOMs or restarts during a 60-photo run.
Verify local OCR at or below 15 seconds p95 after asset loading on a Pixel 6a with Android Chrome, plus 20 consecutive capture/cancel/retry cycles without retained workers or photos.
Measure cold asset loading separately from OCR processing.
The lazy assets have a 30 MiB compressed build budget and are excluded from PWA precaching.
Physical camera permission handling, mobile peak memory, production proxy upload/CSP configuration and paid OpenAI extraction require separate live verification.
The existing progress-photo proxy patch permits sufficiently large uploads, blob previews and WebAssembly compilation; it must be applied before deploying this UI.
Fixture results are a baseline and are not proof that nutrition values are reliable enough to save without review.

Phase 1.5 adds blur guidance using Tenengrad or Laplacian variance on a downscaled preview frame off the main thread.
Sobel output must never become OCR input.
Phase 2 adds automatic table detection and an overlay only if fixture failures show framing is the cause.
Deskew handles rotation and perspective correction handles trapezoids; neither solves curved packaging.
Keep manual crop handles and the explicit AI fallback for curved labels.
Every later phase must beat the previous real PL/EN fixture baseline without increasing incorrect suggestions or exceeding the resource budgets.
